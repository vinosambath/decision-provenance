---
title: Design Review for Order-Fulfillment Interface
date: 2025-05-16
participants: [Victor Nguyen, Tom Reyes, Omar Siddiqui, Leila Haddad, Grace Kim, Ravi Shah]
---

Victor Nguyen: Alright team, thanks for joining. Let’s dive into the design updates for the order-fulfillment interface. Tom, you want to kick us off?

Tom Reyes: Sure! So, I've been working on the shipping status updates. The new layout should be more intuitive, but I’m not sure about the color scheme we chose. It feels a bit... dull?

Omar Siddiqui: Dull? What do you mean? It’s... um, practical?

Leila Haddad: Practical is definitely one way to say it. But Omar, you gotta admit, it could use a pop. We’re not going for an 80s shipping warehouse vibe here.

Ravi Shah: Can we add some neon lights while we’re at it? Maybe a disco ball? 

Grace Kim: Don’t tempt me, Ravi! You know I'd throw that in just to liven up our Monday meetings. But seriously, Tom, maybe we can try a brighter accent color? Just to make it less... gray.

Tom Reyes: Yeah, I was thinking that too. Maybe something that pops but still feels professional? 

Victor Nguyen: Agreed. The last thing we want is for users to feel like they're staring at a rainy day. So, how do we feel about a warm orange or maybe teal? 

Omar Siddiqui: I like teal! Orange always feels too fast-food to me. But teal can be nice... if we don’t overdo it.

Leila Haddad: Just like my coffee! What’s the right amount of teal? Our last design decisions should be a cautionary tale, right? 

Ravi Shah: You mean my bright pinks from last year? I still stand by those.

Grace Kim: Definitely didn’t help anyone take us seriously! But back to the point, we need a color palette that not only looks nice but is functional. So, any thoughts on button placements? 

Tom Reyes: Right! The call-to-action buttons need more visibility. I did some testing, and it looked like half our clicks were just users hunting around for the checkout button.

Omar Siddiqui: Classic case of “where’s Waldo?", huh? 

Leila Haddad: Or *where’s my money* in this case! 

Ravi Shah: Ha! That should be our next branding campaign: “Can’t find the button? Give us your money instead!”

Victor Nguyen: We can vote on that later, Ravi. Seriously, if users can’t find the button, they won’t bother checking out, and that’s our bread and butter. 

Grace Kim: So, should we move the button higher up or just make it bigger?

Tom Reyes: Hmm, bigger might really work. Let’s test it out. Might be something simple like that, but it could make a big difference.

Omar Siddiqui: Who’s going to handle the A/B testing? We need to make sure we’re not just throwing darts at the wall.

Leila Haddad: I can take that on. But I’ll need a couple of days to set it up, so let’s not burn through timelines.

Victor Nguyen: Sounds good. We’ll keep our timelines intact, and we’ll check back next week with updates? 

Ravi Shah: Only if there's cake for the grounding exercise afterward!

Grace Kim: We’ll consider it, but no promises — your dessert antics are legendary.

Victor Nguyen: Alright, let’s wrap it up here. Thanks, team. Looking forward to those updates!
